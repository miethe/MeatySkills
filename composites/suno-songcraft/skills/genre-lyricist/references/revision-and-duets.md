# Revision, lyric variations, and duet dramaturgy

## Keep the edit contract visible

Before changing a supplied song, record protected lines and allowed scope. “Polish” is not permission to replace the narrator, emotional meaning, or chorus. Compare original and revised sung text separately from performance cues. For a keep-words request, reformat or add cues without changing contractions or punctuation.

Offer a small diff table when edits are material: original line, revision, reason, melody-fit uncertainty. Do not manufacture a flaw to justify every change. Some original roughness may be the writer's voice.

## Repair the problem you actually found

| Problem | Repair question |
|---|---|
| Generic feeling statement | What action or object would let the listener infer the feeling? |
| Verse two repeats verse one | What happens, changes, or becomes newly known? |
| Forced rhyme | What would the speaker say without needing the end word? |
| Pronoun drift | Who does each “you,” “we,” and “they” refer to here? |
| Mixed metaphor | Are the physical actions compatible, or is the mismatch intentional? |
| Crowded phrase | Which words carry meaning and stress, and which can go? |
| Unearned redemption | What does the narrator do differently? |
| Decorative regionalism | Would removing the place/object alter the meaning? |
| Weak bridge | What new angle becomes possible only after the earlier sections? |
| Polished but empty | What precise, risky admission is being avoided? |

## Distinct voices

Create a two-line note for each singer: what they want and what they cannot yet admit. Give them different knowledge or tactics, not merely different vocal registers. They can share vocabulary because of a relationship while still differing in perspective.

Useful structures include:

- **Parallel accounts:** each singer tells a side; the chorus exposes agreement or contradiction.
- **Question and answer:** one makes a claim; the other complicates or resolves it.
- **Time-separated selves:** voices represent before/after, with clear framing.
- **Converging confession:** each accepts responsibility before they share a conclusion.

Do not assume one character comforts while the other acts because of gender. Do not let a backing ensemble become a new narrator accidentally. Backing lyrics, when present, should be supplied as actual sung words with clear cue placement, not merely described as “answers.”

For the Carolina example, reciprocal bridge answers work because both leads move from private vulnerability into a shared statement. That is a dramaturgical choice, not a template for every duet.

## Harmony as meaning

Use solo lines for distinct perspective; call-and-response for interaction; unison for a common declaration; harmony for compatible meanings; a withheld harmony for tension. These are expressive options, not fixed semantics. A deliberate mismatch can be powerful if the brief wants it.

For a text-to-music destination, request a simple version first. Section-sized assignments are easier to evaluate than a dense line-by-line score. Do not imply exact independent singer control from cue labels.

## Alternatives that teach something

Change one lyric dimension per variation: more literal versus more imagistic; intimate versus communal addressee; present action versus memory; unresolved versus redemptive ending; compact versus expansive hook. Hold the premise and arrangement stable unless the user asks for a new direction.

Label whether an alternative is a drop-in line, verse replacement, alternate hook, or full lyric version. Without the source melody, call drop-in suitability provisional. Never mix two different versions into a single unlabeled final lyric block.

## Final audit

Check protected words, perspective, tense, section development, image coherence, rhyme integrity, diction, breath, hook vowels, audience fit, and cue separation. State only the uncertain items that would materially change the next draft or recording.
